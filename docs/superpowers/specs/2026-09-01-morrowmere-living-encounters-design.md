# MORROWMERE — Living Encounters and Cinematic Dialogue Design

**Status:** Approved for implementation  
**Scope:** Chronicle I — The Black Banner  
**Product language:** English  
**Design date:** 2026-09-01

## 1. Outcome

Chronicle I remains a grounded medieval sword-and-sorcery adventure, but it stops feeling like a sequence of illustrated pages. The existing 332 authored scenes stay in place as the campaign spine. A new living-encounter layer turns travel, investigation, social meetings, hazards, and combat setups into connected playable sequences with visible stakes, real checks, immediate consequences, follow-up scenes, distinct rewards, and companion reactions.

The player should always understand:

1. where they are and why they are there;
2. what changed since the previous scene;
3. who is present in the illustration;
4. what each choice attempts, without revealing future surprises;
5. whether the result succeeded, failed, caused combat, spent a resource, gained a reward, or opened a later consequence;
6. why the next scene follows naturally.

The expansion is additive. It must not discard the current long-form plot, combat engine, inventory, equipment, merchant, leveling, save, chapter restart, or procedural director.

## 2. Experience principles

### 2.1 Concrete adventure first

The early campaign is easy to follow: two medicine wagons leave Dunmere for Greywatch, the road becomes unsafe, evidence does not match the apparent attackers, and the player must decide whom to trust. Political and supernatural complexity grows gradually in later chapters.

Narration uses concrete actions, locations, and motives. Most paragraphs are one to three sentences. Poetic language is reserved for chapter openings, rare relics, visions, and emotional turning points.

### 2.2 Every choice does something

An interactive choice must produce at least one of these outcomes:

- a stat check with distinct success and failure branches;
- immediate combat or a meaningful combat setup advantage;
- health, focus, gold, XP, item, faction, evidence, threat, or tension change;
- a named character's trust or quest state change;
- a queued authored follow-up;
- a route or later scene becoming available or unavailable.

A choice cannot exist only to change an invisible flag and return to an unrelated random page. Pure exposition is presented as a short dialogue or transition beat, not as a fake choice.

### 2.3 Consequences are shown, then carried forward

After a choice, the result panel names the attempted action and shows concise feedback such as:

- `Cunning succeeded · 72% chance`
- `Critical failure · Ambush`
- `+18 XP`, `-4 Health`, `Lockpick consumed`, `Mara approves`

The next call to action is contextual: `Enter the tollhouse`, `Speak with Mara`, `Draw your sword`, `Return to the road`, or `Continue to Greywatch`. Generic `Continue` is retained only for neutral bridge passages where no more precise label is possible.

### 2.4 Procedural does not mean disconnected

The director may vary optional encounters, enemies, loot, road conditions, and companion moments, but it cannot break cause and effect. Each expedition is built as connected packets:

`arrival/setup → choice or dialogue → resolved consequence → optional battle/reward → aftermath → travel bridge → next anchor`

Queued follow-ups take priority over unrelated random scenes. Main-story anchors remain ordered and cannot be skipped.

## 3. Chronicle I macro-flow

The existing eight-chapter plot remains canonical.

| Chapter | Core adventure | Complexity growth | Living encounter emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The Greywatch Road | Escort two medicine wagons and survive the road to Greywatch | Clear local danger and first contradictory evidence | Travel problems, roadside people, Mara introduction, ambushes, wagon preservation |
| 2. Raiders at Dawn | Defend Greywatch and trace royal weapons | Possible help from inside the walls | Town defence, witnesses, infiltrators, faction trust, Mara recruitment path |
| 3. The Redwater Trail | Pursue the supply chain beyond the border | Human and orc motives diverge from official claims | Tracking, negotiation, river hazards, prisoner choices, Rukhar chain |
| 4. Ashes Under Stone | Enter the old mines and expose the hidden forge | Magic becomes a practical military threat | Exploration, traps, ore, mages, environmental combat, Lyra chain |
| 5. The Hollow Crown | Cross divided territory to find the command behind the raids | The conspiracy reaches nobles and old royal claims | Espionage, disguises, rival agents, difficult alliances, Caldus chain |
| 6. The Broken Oath | Survive betrayal and the siege of Greywatch | Earlier loyalties determine who opens or holds gates | Siege decisions, companion crises, resource triage, multi-stage battles |
| 7. Roads to Crownless Keep | Assemble a route and coalition for the final march | Factions demand incompatible prices | High-level route checks, rare monsters, alliance tests, Talla culmination |
| 8. The Black Banner | Enter Crownless Keep and expose or seize the truth | Every major choice converges | Boss chains, evidence use, companion endings, multiple epilogues |

### 3.1 Chapter pacing budget

Each chapter keeps approximately seven mandatory anchors and gains a pool of 20–30 living encounters. A normal run sees 10–16 of those optional encounters, selected by route, flags, level, party, and seed.

Per chapter target:

- 4–6 combat opportunities, including avoidable, mandatory, and consequence-triggered fights;
- 3–5 dialogue or companion sequences;
- 2–4 recovery, merchant, crafting, or safe-hub moments;
- 4–7 checks across Strength, Cunning, and Will;
- 2–4 callbacks to earlier choices;
- no more than two exposition-only transitions in a row.

A first completion should now be roughly 18–24 hours. Alternate routes, failed checks, unrecruited companions, hidden evidence, and rare encounters should support 35–50 hours of meaningful discovery without repetitive grinding.

## 4. Chapter 1 detailed sequence

Chapter 1 is the first fully enhanced content pass and establishes the grammar used by Chapters 2–8.

### Phase A — Leaving Dunmere

The opening cinematic ends on the same dawn road shown by the first playable scene. The camp/home page uses a wide, bright caravan illustration with only the player and workers established by the narration. Route descriptions explain the character of each road without listing future merchants, companions, relics, or encounter types.

The player inspects the manifest, meets wagon master Jory Fen, and chooses how to prepare the damaged rear wagon. Strength may secure the load quickly; Cunning may redistribute it; spending gold may buy a replacement fitting. Each result changes the first travel packet.

### Phase B — The first miles and Mara Vey

Small road events teach checks and consequences: a washed verge, a missing mule, suspicious cart tracks, a blocked ford, or nervous draft horses. These are grounded problems, not filler. Success can preserve time, medicine, or focus. Failure can cost health, supplies, or raise threat.

Mara appears alone beside a cut milestone in art that matches the scene. Her meeting is a cinematic dialogue sequence:

1. environmental establishing beat;
2. Mara identifies herself and gives one verifiable observation;
3. the player may question her, test her claim, accept her path, or keep distance;
4. Mara remembers the manner of the response;
5. the selected road immediately leads to an authored follow-up showing whether her warning mattered.

She is not recruited in this chapter. The Allies page shows only recruited allies; unrecruited character histories remain hidden in the Codex, and recruitment requirements are never spoiled.

### Phase C — The empty tollhouse

The caravan reaches an abandoned tollhouse. Investigation choices can expose the torn register, hidden cellar, removed Greywatch badges, or a false trail. A failed Cunning check can spring a wire alarm and bring scouts or raiders. A forceful entry may bypass the lock but destroy evidence. Spending a tool or asking Mara for help changes the follow-up rather than producing identical text.

This phase includes at least one optional battle, one noncombat evidence path, and one rare cache with a distinctive early-game item.

### Phase D — First blood on the road

The first organized attack is a real multi-enemy battle. The preceding choice determines setup:

- shield the driver: better wagon outcome, player begins exposed;
- take the high bank: ranged advantage, delayed rescue;
- cut a wheel loose as cover: easier first wave, damaged transport afterward;
- detect the signal early with Cunning: chance to avoid the opening volley.

The setup scene always enters combat when appropriate. Victory enters the reward screen, grants XP and encounter loot, then opens a specific aftermath scene. Defeat follows the existing expedition-loss rules.

### Phase E — The burning bridge

The bridge sequence joins combat, rescue, and Mara's values. The player cannot save every objective without prior preparation. Choices affect civilians, medicine, the wounded toll officer, Mara's trust, and the condition of the convoy. The aftermath explicitly describes what survived and queues a later reaction.

### Phase F — The staged banner

The player finds apparent orc evidence that is too clean. Strength can lift wreckage to reach a corpse before rain ruins the scene. Cunning can compare boot marks and lashings. Will can press a frightened witness for a coherent account without losing control of the crowd. Failure does not stop the campaign; it creates a different, less reliable evidence state and makes later persuasion harder.

### Phase G — The last miles

Previously saved people, wagon condition, Mara's trust, and threat determine the encounters before Greywatch. Optional packets include a pursuit, a wounded woodcutter, a closed track, a choice between a witness and medicine, and a final push uphill. Repeated characters acknowledge earlier decisions.

### Phase H — Greywatch gate

The gate scene resolves through evidence, a difficult check, a fight caused by arriving pursuers, or a costly concession. The art accurately shows the surviving wagons, current allies, visible injuries, and time of day. Chapter 2 begins from the exact entry condition rather than resetting the fiction.

## 5. Living encounter families

Each family has a setup, at least two meaningful approaches, a resolution, and an optional follow-up.

1. **Travel problem:** weather, damaged transport, blocked road, navigation, animal trouble.
2. **Social encounter:** travelers, soldiers, refugees, scouts, smugglers, villagers, prisoners.
3. **Investigation:** tracks, documents, bodies, camps, forged insignia, hidden rooms.
4. **Hazard and trap:** snares, unstable bridges, mine gas, floodwater, fire, magical residue.
5. **Creature encounter:** goblin, orc, beast, monster, or rare strange event with talk, avoid, exploit, or fight options where plausible.
6. **Moral pressure:** protect people, preserve mission supplies, honor a promise, reveal evidence, accept a compromise.
7. **Companion sequence:** dialogue, personal problem, loyalty test, injury, disagreement, or recruitment milestone.
8. **Merchant and recovery:** trade, repair, treatment, campcraft, rumors, and specialist services.
9. **Combat setup and aftermath:** choose formation or objective, enter battle, claim rewards, then see the consequence in the world.

Rare strange events remain understandable. Their immediate facts are clear even when their full cause is mysterious.

### 5.1 Rare sword-and-sorcery encounters

Chapter 1 also establishes a low-frequency pool of vivid encounters that makes Morrowmere feel stranger and more dangerous than an ordinary medieval road. These scenes use recognizable physical details and immediate stakes even when their full explanation remains hidden. The first pool includes:

- a goblin hanging from an old warning tree, with evidence that the executioners may not have been human;
- a sealed suit of battlefield armor that moves only when the medicine wagon draws close;
- the abandoned edge of a goblin war camp, where wounded enemies, stolen family goods, and an officer's coded order create incompatible choices;
- a sword driven into a roadside grave mound, valuable but bound to a practical curse;
- a collapsed shrine whose protective ward is still fighting the creature trapped beneath it;
- a monster feeding beside a ruined siege engine that can be distracted, trapped, bargained past, or fought for a unique reward.

Each concept is an authored three-scene packet: discovery, playable confrontation, and consequence-specific aftermath. At least two approaches use a real stat check, battle remains possible where the fiction supports it, and a clever noncombat solution can earn different—not automatically better—loot. Illustrations must show the defining object or creature clearly and use a genuinely distinct composition. Violence may be ominous but is not graphically indulgent.

## 6. Real stat checks

### 6.1 Check model

Choices may declare a check using one primary stat: Strength, Cunning, or Will.

Base success chance:

`55 + (effective stat - difficulty) × 10`

The final chance is clamped to 15–95%. Gear tags, active companion abilities, injuries, route preparation, evidence, and earlier flags can add bounded modifiers. The UI shows the final percentage before selection but does not reveal the exact success and failure rewards.

The roll is deterministic for a save seed, scene ID, visit count, and choice ID. Reloading cannot reroll the same attempt. Starting a new expedition or restarting a chapter produces a new route seed.

Outcome bands:

- critical success: roll 1–5 and the check succeeds;
- success: roll is at or below chance;
- failure: roll is above chance;
- critical failure: roll 96–100 and the check fails.

Critical bands only apply when the authored choice supplies critical effects or text; otherwise they use the normal success or failure branch.

### 6.2 Branch contract

A checked choice declares:

- stat and difficulty;
- optional visible situational modifiers;
- success text and effects;
- failure text and effects;
- optional critical text/effects;
- optional success/failure next scene IDs;
- optional success/failure combat encounter IDs.

Every referenced scene, item, companion, and encounter is validated at build time.

## 7. Combat integration and rewards

All existing authored scenes with `type: 'combat'` and an `encounterId` must enter the referenced battle after the setup choice. The choice first applies its tactical effects, then combat starts. This restores all 48 currently dormant authored battles.

Combat setup effects may include:

- initiative or opening guard;
- enemy stagger or exposed status;
- player injury or lost focus;
- protected civilian/wagon objective;
- reinforcements, retreat option, or altered enemy group;
- post-battle loot or aftermath flags.

Victory follows the existing battle → reward → story pipeline. Encounter-specific rewards may add gold, XP, a consumable, crafting material, or a unique item. Rare and boss items are tied to an authored opponent or difficult branch, not generic random drops.

Noncombat checks and quests can award XP. Repeating the same encounter family gives diminishing XP and cannot push the player far beyond the chapter's soft level band.

Consumables remain usable inside and outside battle. Equipment choices can modify checks when their tags logically apply.

## 8. Cinematic dialogue

### 8.1 Presentation

A dialogue scene contains short beats rather than a wall of prose:

- full-width environment art;
- a consistent standing or half-body character layer when someone is speaking;
- speaker name;
- one to three sentences per beat;
- tap anywhere to reveal skip controls during voiced milestone sequences;
- player response choices after the final beat.

The scene background remains unique to the event. Character layers may reuse a canonical face/outfit reference for visual consistency, but pose, lighting, expression, and placement must match the event. No unrecruited companion appears as a party member in travel art.

The full opening cinematic, every main-story anchor, and every authored rare sword-and-sorcery encounter use English voice-over generated through ElevenLabs from the final locked narration. Main companion milestones may also use character voice-over. Ordinary procedural travel dialogue remains text-first so pacing stays fast and the package remains practical. Provider credentials are read only from a local environment secret during production and are never written to source, generated manifests, logs, or release files.

### 8.2 Dialogue memory

Responses can adjust trust, loyalty, faction standing, evidence, flags, or queued follow-ups. Later lines acknowledge meaningful earlier decisions. Dialogue never exposes exact recruitment recipes or future plot twists.

## 9. Companion paths

Mara, Talla, Rukhar, Caldus, and Lyra each receive a 12–18 event chain spread across introduction, testing, cooperation, personal crisis, recruitment opportunity, loyalty conflict, and ending consequence.

Recruitment must require several compatible actions, not one obvious button. A companion can respect a difficult disagreement if the player acted consistently. Poor loyalty can cause refusal, departure, a harder battle, or a different ending, but not an unexplained disappearance.

The Allies page lists only recruited companions and current actionable status. Longer histories belong in the Codex after discovery. Hidden requirements stay hidden.

## 10. Art direction and asset budget

The current Morrowmere look is retained: readable grounded fantasy, cinematic natural light, clean surfaces, believable clothing and weapons, and clear subjects. New art must have:

- no grain overlays, random dots, scan lines, illegible symbols, text, watermark, or unexplained artifacts;
- enough brightness and contrast for phone screens;
- accurate location, weather, time, characters, party size, wounds, equipment, and action;
- a composition that leaves safe space for readable UI;
- a unique event background, not a reused scene with a different title.

The 332 existing unique scene illustrations remain unless narration accuracy requires replacement. New living encounters target 200–250 additional unique environment images plus a controlled set of consistent character layers and combat variants, producing 500–600 distinct scene compositions overall.

Android delivery remains under the Play Store package target. New stills use high-quality WebP with a practical average size budget, and nonessential high-resolution variants are excluded from the base bundle. If the final bundle approaches the delivery ceiling, later-chapter media moves to Play Asset Delivery rather than lowering readability.

## 11. Audio and haptics

Music is organized by location and tension state, with smooth crossfades instead of unrelated restarts. Cinematic narration, image timing, and music cues share authored timestamps.

The opening is mixed as one continuous film cue. It uses a coherent dark medieval-orchestral theme, restrained natural road/wind/fire ambience, and only story-motivated impacts. Music ducks beneath spoken narration and returns between lines. No decorative whoosh, synthetic chirp, random hit, or unrelated creature sound may be added merely to fill silence. Each opening panel declares narration start/end, image transition, music state, ambience, and optional SFX in one cue sheet, and the final waveform/timeline is reviewed against that sheet.

Main-story and rare-event narration uses the exact final on-screen English copy, split at natural sentence boundaries. Visual transitions follow sentence timestamps; they do not advance in the middle of a sentence. Dialogue voice and narrator voice remain clearly distinct where both are used.

New feedback cues cover:

- choice selection;
- skill success, critical success, failure, and critical failure;
- ambush transition;
- weapon hit, miss, critical, block, parry, spell, enemy defeat, and victory;
- item gain, equipment change, purchase, treatment, and companion approval/disapproval.

Haptics are short and distinct: light for choice, medium for hit/success, sharp double pulse for critical, and heavier bounded pulse for player damage or failure. Settings control music, SFX, voice, and haptics independently.

## 12. Save, migration, and failure behavior

Save state persists:

- queued follow-up scene IDs;
- check attempt records and deterministic results;
- current dialogue beat;
- pending combat setup and aftermath;
- visit counts used by deterministic checks;
- current route seed and chapter snapshot.

Existing saves migrate without losing campaign, inventory, equipment, gold, companions, or chapter progress. If a queued scene no longer exists after an update, migration drops only that invalid queue entry and records a recoverable diagnostic instead of blocking the player.

Defeat and terminal story states always show clear actions: retry from camp, restart chapter, or return to main menu. No state may end on an unresponsive page.

## 13. Content validation and acceptance rules

Focused validation rejects:

- combat scenes without a valid encounter;
- checked choices without both success and failure outcomes;
- dangling next-scene or callback IDs;
- mandatory anchors that cannot be reached;
- follow-up chains with no exit;
- duplicate illustration mappings or missing files;
- an interactive choice with no tangible state, branch, battle, or relationship effect;
- dialogue with no speaker or empty beat;
- art metadata that includes a companion not present in scene state;
- route descriptions that spoil encounter categories, rewards, companions, or relics.

Release acceptance requires:

1. a real Chapter 1 route can demonstrate check success, check failure, avoidable combat, mandatory combat, item reward, XP gain, companion reaction, and a callback;
2. all 48 existing authored combat scenes activate their battles;
3. combat victory returns through reward to the correct authored aftermath;
4. current saves migrate and load;
5. every new scene has an accurate unique composition and readable copy;
6. no scene chain produces the previous `No eligible Chronicle scene` dead end;
7. the focused validator, reducer tests, Chapter 1 route tests, and Android build complete successfully.
